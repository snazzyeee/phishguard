02 / THEORY & ARCHITECTURE
How the system works
Queue mechanics, scoring rules, and performance in one place.

        

          
THE CORE IDEA
Reclaim every slot with modulo arithmetic.
A plain array queue leaves freed positions behind as its front moves forward. In this circular queue, both pointers wrap to index 0 after index 9. The array never shifts, and emails remain in arrival order.
ENQUEUErear = (rear + 1) % MAX
DEQUEUEfront = (front + 1) % MAX
CAPACITYMAX = 10

          
STATE DETECTION
Count removes ambiguity.
Pointer positions alone cannot reliably distinguish a full ring from an empty one. The explicit count variable resolves both states.
count == 0 EMPTYcount == 10 FULL

          
Follow one email through the C program.
  1. Store it. struct Email holds the ID, sender, subject, and body. Ten records live in queue[MAX]; front, rear, and count describe which slots are in use.
  2. Receive it. receiveEmail() checks isFull(), reads the fields, then calls enqueue(). The rear moves with (rear + 1) % 10; the new record goes in that slot and count increases.
  3. Assess it. assessNext() checks isEmpty() and calls dequeue(). It removes the oldest record at front, advances front with (front + 1) % 10, and decreases count. report() then scores that email.
  4. Explain the verdict. assess() checks the subject and body for keywords and http://, then checks the sender. verdict() maps the capped score to SAFE, SUSPICIOUS, or PHISHING.

          
Why the queue wraps around.
Fill all 10 slots, then assess the first 8 emails. Now front = 8, rear = 9, and count = 2. Receive two more emails: rear moves from 9 to 0, then 0 to 1. The pending order is slots 8 → 9 → 0 → 1, so the oldest email is still assessed first. Use More controls → Wrap-around demo in the simulator to see this state.
queue[(front + i) % 10] is also how displayQueue() traverses pending emails in FIFO order without moving the array elements.

          
SCORING PIPELINE
Explain every verdict.
The subject and body are scanned for 12 keyword substrings. An insecure link adds 15 points; a malformed sender or risky domain adds 20. The final score is capped at 100.
Try the bank sample: 8 distinct keywords × 10 = 80, plus 15 for http:// and 20 for the .xyz sender. The raw 115 becomes 100, so the verdict is PHISHING. This is a fixed rule set, not a learned model.
 0–29 SAFE 30–59 SUSPICIOUS 60–100 PHISHING

PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS

n = pending emails (at most 10); L = email text length. Queue storage is fixed at 10 email records.

          
What to point out during the demo.
  • FIFO: Peek shows the oldest pending email without removing it; Assess next removes that same email.
  • Overflow and underflow: A full queue rejects a new email, and an empty queue cannot be assessed. The checks happen before the array is read or written.
  • Two independent jobs: The circular queue controls processing order. The scoring rules classify an email after it leaves the queue.
  • Fixed storage: The queue uses 10 struct Email records regardless of how many messages have been processed over the whole session. Front and rear reuse those slots.

          
ADVANTAGES
Small, fair, and inspectable.
  • FIFO handling preserves arrival order.
  • Freed array slots are reused without shifting.
  • Overflow and underflow are explicit.
  • Every score can be explained by rules.
